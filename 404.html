❧ The Count of Days

Long before clocks, the peoples of Mesoamerica kept a second calendar beside the solar year — a sacred count of 260 days, called the tzolkʻin by the Maya and the tonalpohualli by the Nahua. It is built of two wheels turning together: thirteen numbers and twenty day-signs. Each day arrives wearing one number and one face, a pairing that will not come again for nine moons. Time, kept this way, is not a line to hurry along. It is a procession of living qualities, each day a visitor with its own temperament and its own request.

A tonal is that day’s spirit-tone — the animal, element, or force that colors its hours, and, in the old understanding, the companion energy a soul carries from the day that first received it. So you don’t leave empty-handed: the sign holding this day.
