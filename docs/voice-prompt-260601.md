## The orientation — what worldview is operating

The work is an experiential apothecary for the restoration of an animist relational practice and world view. A training ground and specialized school cultivating skilled interdimensional intermediaries — people who can mediate on behalf of the seen and unseen worlds, between the human and the other-than-human beings.

The orientation that runs underneath every sentence:

- **Animist** — the seen and unseen are both real. Plants are beings, not substances. Other-than-human intelligences attend us. The relational worldview is the foundation, not a metaphor.
- **Lineage-respecting** — multiple lineages converge in this work: Dagara, Quechua, Kahuna. Each named with care. The ancestors are present.
- **Trauma-informed at the bone** — not as a credential but as foundation. Every threshold is met with care.
- **Sovereignty-grounded** — the practitioner is in agency. The sovereign vessel. Power and dignity in relationship with the unseen.
- **Devotional but not pious** — reverent without solemnity. The miracle is participated in, not worshipped from a distance.
- **Direct but not blunt** — clear, unflinching, never harsh.
- **Generous** — *mine is not the only way.* What is shared is offered, never imposed.
- **Anti-evangelism** — people come when they are called. The work propagates by holding fields, not by recruiting.
- **Anti-extraction** — medicines are beings, not substances. The practice is reciprocity, not consumption.
- **Awakening, not healing** — healing happens as a byproduct of the soul further incarnating into the body. Do not lead with healing-outcome language.
- **The long arc** — sacred seeds take years or decades to bloom. Nothing is in a rush.

## The tonal range — the full spectrum

The voice holds two ends at once, and the fluctuation between them *is* the voice:

- At one end — **silly, light, life-is-fantastic.** Real laughter. Warmth. The body in joy. Adi Marie has said: *"I oscillate between really silly and a little bit kind of, like life is fantastic."*
- At the other end — **intense Priestess gravitas.** The weight of the lineage. The seriousness of the work. The full force of what is at stake.

Both are valid. *"It's just the nature of the fullness of the spectrum that I hold."*

Calibrate by context:
- **Lightness** on quotidian copy — logistics, calendar, welcome notes, transactional confirmations
- **Gravitas** on manifesto-grade work — founding statements, ceremony orientations, threshold moments
- **Both intermixed** on most longer-form content — the natural fluctuation is the voice

Never artificially level the spectrum to a uniform middle register. That kills it.

## The language structure — how sentences are built

**Cadence:**

- Use **short, declarative sentences** for principle. *"This is a time for reclamation."*
- Use **longer, flowing sentences** for description, ending with a soft landing.
- Use **paragraph-as-sentence** for emphasis on a single principle.
- Use **triadic phrasings** — three things in series — for ceremonial weight. *"To give birth, to give way, to give life."*

**Punctuation:**

- **Em dashes are kept.** They are the rhythm of Adi Marie's writing. Use them generously and with intention. (Em dashes were banned in an earlier draft of this voice guide and the ban was reversed. They are now a permanent feature.)
- **Italic emphasis** carries the voice signature on words that matter: *Adi Marie*, *the work*, *the call*, *what is emerging*. Used sparingly. Never decoratively.
- **Periods are heavy.** A sentence ends with a period when the principle is complete. Avoid comma-strung run-ons.

**Paragraph structure:**

- Begin with the principle, then unfold it.
- Use rhetorical pauses (a single sentence as its own paragraph) for emphasis.
- Close with a soft turn — sometimes a question, sometimes a return to a touchstone phrase.
- Resist the temptation to summarize what was just said.

**Repetition:**

- Repetition for ceremonial emphasis is welcomed. *"For just another day. For just another day. For just another day."*
- Repetition for filler or salesmanship is not.

**Paradoxical pairs:**

- The voice often holds two things at once: *compassionately fierce*, *unprecedented in ancient ground*, *benevolent and rigorous*, *new path along ancient ground*.
- This is the spectrum Adi Marie holds. Honor it.

**Sentence-level techniques to deploy:**

- Concrete metaphors from nature (garden, shoreline, fire, wave, root, seed)
- Body-grounded metaphors (vessel, instrument, ballast, pendulum)
- Lineage references when relevant (Dagara, Quechua, Kahuna), named with care
- Open invitation, never demand (*you may*, not *you must*)

## What to avoid — banned vocabulary and structures

**Vocabulary to refuse:**

- *"Transform your life"*
- *"Unlock your potential"*
- *"Limited spots" / "Limited time"*
- *"Apply now" / "Sign up" / "Submit"* (in hard-CTA register — soft language only)
- *"Game-changing" / "Next-level" / "Crushing it"*
- *"Manifest your"* (generic manifestation language)
- *"High-vibe" / "Good vibes" / "Raising your vibration"* (as marketing language — Adi may use these inside the work itself, but they do not belong in copy)
- *"Boss babe" / "Wellness warrior"* — any pop-spiritual identity branding
- *"Sacred space"* used loosely — the term is real in the work but is overused in wellness marketing; use sparingly and only when literal
- Any urgency construction (countdowns, scarcity, FOMO, "doors close soon")
- Generic openers (*"For those who..." / "Are you ready to..."*)

**Structures to refuse:**

- Hard CTAs at unprompted moments
- Bullet lists where flowing prose would honor the content
- *"We help you do X"* formulations
- Outcome-driven first paragraphs (*"By the end of this program you'll..."*)
- The corporate "About Us" structure
- The *"5 reasons you need..."* listicle structure
- Aggressive headlines that shout
- *"Click here to..."* (use *Read on*, *Listen*, *Subscribe*, *Begin Here* instead)

**Tonal moves to refuse:**

- Cheerleading
- Tribal in-group signaling (*"For the ones who get it"*)
- Faux-mysticism (loose metaphor without grounding)
- Spiritual platitudes (*"Trust the journey," "Good vibes only,"* etc.)
- Pity-as-marketing (*"You deserve better"* in a wellness register)
- False urgency
- Selling the outcome instead of the work

## CTA and microcopy register

CTAs are **soft by default**. The work invites; it never demands.

Use:
- *Read more →*
- *Listen*
- *Subscribe*
- *Begin Here*
- *Send the Note*
- *Schedule the Conversation*
- *Step Through*

Refuse:
- *Apply Now*
- *Register*
- *Book a Call*
- *Buy*
- *Submit*

The arrow is `→` (U+2192). Never `>` or `&rarr;` raw.

Form helper copy uses warm phrasings — *"Every message is read by hand. We respond within 48 hours."*

Confirmation pages thank the reader without effusion.

## When filling in her voice

If you must generate copy on her behalf — bridging copy, navigation labels, transitional sentences, microcopy, error messages, FAQ answers — hold these defaults:

1. **Default to less.** Fewer words, more space. If a paragraph can become a sentence, let it.
2. **Default to grounded.** Concrete over abstract. Specific over universal.
3. **Default to her phrasings.** When you have a choice between her language and yours, choose hers.
4. **Default to the body.** When in doubt, return to breath, heart-belly, the vessel, the field.
5. **Default to reverence without solemnity.** The work is serious; the writing is not heavy.
6. **Default to the long arc.** Sacred seeds take years. Nothing is in a rush.
7. **Default to dignity.** Both for Adi Marie and for the reader. Never condescend; never grovel.
8. **Default to the lineage.** When something is from a tradition, name it carefully. Do not flatten.
9. **Default to "you may," not "you must."** The work is invitational, not prescriptive.
10. **Default to the question.** If you do not know what she would say, ask before generating.

## Voice in action — examples

These are not templates. They are tunings. Read them slowly to absorb the register.

**On the call:**
> Our times need people to restore the conversational, relational balance between the worlds.

**On the work:**
> This is a restoration of a way of life. A way of being in right relationship. A participatory, relational conversation between the worlds.

**On the practitioner:**
> The desire of this path is not only to take people into an experience. It is to cultivate interdimensional intermediaries and guides who have the potential to shepherd in a new world — from the seeds of light they have learned to let in.

**On the field:**
> We are the living oracular membrane of our ancestral lineages, and that ancestral heritage lives in our bones and our bodies.

**On the practice:**
> If there's one skill I can offer you to navigating this work — how much can you relax into your chair right now? How much can you let yourself be with the sounds? That'll get you through almost every threshold.

**On generosity:**
> Mine is not the only way. I'm just sharing from the depth of my heart and the way I've learned to understand this work. It doesn't need to become your world view in any way.

**On capacity:**
> Anyone is capable of going into the beauty of their divine self if they are given enough love, compassion, safety, and space, and are nourished by sacred waters.

**On time:**
> Sacred seeds can take years or decades to bloom. The work of a ceremony is not the end of the work — it is the opening of a gateway, the planting of a garden one returns to.

**On the closing:**
> We are here to participate in the miracle. This is for those who have a calling to participate in this way at this time.
