Chapter Two

The manifesto

Our times need people to restore the conversational, relational balance between the worlds.

We are at an urgent time. The work is to resituate ourselves down from a position of supremacy, and to take our place as a guardian and stewardship species. We are here to be in service.

The Animist Apothecary is an experiential apothecary for the restoration of an animist relational practice and world view. It is a training ground and a specialized school, cultivating skilled interdimensional intermediaries — people who can mediate on behalf of the seen and unseen worlds, between the human and the other-than-human beings.

This is a restoration of a way of life. A way of being in right relationship. A participatory, relational conversation between the worlds.

We are the living oracular membrane of our ancestral lineages and traditions. The inheritance of our living lineage — our ancestral heritage — is within our bones. It is within our bodies.

We are here to continue. To give birth, to give way, to give life. To the continuation of lineages, of tradition, of innovation, of culture — in response to the gravity of our times.

These are the grounds and opportunities within which to sit in tutelage of the other worlds — their teachings, their initiations, their attunements. A place to formalize and embody procedural ritual technology and ceremonial practice. To engage interdimensionally with skill, poise, rapport, and the artisan’s touch of inspirationally skilled mastery.

We are not only meant to open to those energies. We are meant to anchor them down. That is what this world needs at this time — an anchoring down of a new reality we are shaping and co-creating together.

The desire of this path is not only to take people into an experience. It is to cultivate interdimensional intermediaries and guides who have the potential to shepherd in a new world — from the seeds of light they have learned to let in.

This work is, in some way, unprecedented in the form Adi Marie was taught — and at the same time, deeply in integrity with what the spirit world has been showing. New paths can rest on ancient ground.

We are here to participate in the miracle.
This is for those who have a calling to participate in this way at this time.

Chapter Three

I believe

On StewardshipI believe humans were entrusted as a guardian and stewardship species.
On the Medicine PeopleI believe that across the world, a percentage of the population has been particularly and uniquely skilled from birth — with an emergence of gifts that predispositions them to develop into the medicine people on behalf of the many scopes and valencies of reality and the relationships between them.
On DiasporaI believe that in a post-imperial culture, many of us are born into diaspora communities where the roots of oral tradition and lineage have been intentionally severed, muted, and destroyed. We are born without the elders to help us cultivate and hone our innate gifts and skills.
On the HourI believe this is a time for reclamation.
On Your MedicineI believe you have the inherent wisdom and attunement of the medicine that is yours to walk with in this world.
On Soul & Star FamiliesI believe there are families of ancient lineage and power that have been woven in flesh and bone to hold and to receive — lineages of soul and star families. The merging of earth and stars is how we bring in a new world through devotionally skilled discipline and practice.
On the Living MembraneI believe we are the living oracular membrane of our ancestral lineages, and that ancestral heritage lives in our bones and our bodies. We are here to give birth, to give way, to give life — to the continuation of lineages, of tradition, of innovation, of culture, in response to the gravity of our times.
On AwakeningI believe awakening, not healing, is the principal work. Healing happens as a byproduct of the soul further incarnating into the body. These are teachers — the plants, the practices, the unseen — who are here to facilitate awakening. By the nature of awakening, healing occurs. You do not have to hunt for it.
On the Plants as BeingsI believe the plants and medicines are beings — spirits, personified entities — not substances. They are deeply benevolent forces beyond humankind, attending to our evolution. They love us beyond our comprehension.
On Our Long PracticeI believe humans have been sitting with entheogens since the very beginning. Arguably this is part of why our species occupies the position it does on this planet.
On AnchoringI believe the work is not only to open to other worlds, but to anchor what comes through down into this one — to hold the resonance, hold the field, and implement it here.
On CeremonyI believe ceremony is a procedural ritual technology. It can be learned, practiced, refined, and embodied — with the discipline and the capacity to stay attuned to the essence and purity of the heart, so that what comes through is only goodness, healing, harmony, restoration, peace, balance, and deep cleansing of this world and all its inhabitants, seen and unseen.
On Direct ExperienceI believe direct experience with the Divine is some of the most powerful work a human being can do.
On Stewardship of the FieldI believe this work is a form of spiritual ceremonial stewardship — that we are not only practitioners receiving wisdom, but contributors to a collective field of wisdom that others draw upon when they enter similar space.
On Unprecedented GroundI believe what I am cultivating is, in some way, unprecedented in the form I was taught — and at the same time, deeply in integrity with what the spirit world has been showing me. New paths can rest on ancient ground.
On CapacityI believe anyone is capable of going into the beauty of their divine self if they are given enough love, compassion, safety, and space, and are nourished by sacred waters. I have come from a hard life, and that is precisely why I share what I share — to propagate more of that divine light into this world.
On the Sword of MercyI believe we are meant to be compassionately fierce. There is a reason archangels carry swords. We are shepherds, and stewardship sometimes requires protection of the goodness.
On Sacred SeedsI believe sacred seeds can take years or decades to bloom. The work of a ceremony, an initiation, or any deep practice is not the end — it is the opening of a gateway, the planting of a garden one returns to.
On Fallen SacramentsI believe sacred plant medicines have been gifted to humankind across millennia, and that many of them — cannabis, the poppy, coca — have suffered when interfaced with outside the contexts that kept them sacred. The spirit of a plant can be shaken and shaped by how it is treated.
On DiscernmentI believe medicine makes you pay the price up front and reap the benefits later. Intoxicants give the relief first and ask for the price later.
On the CallI believe we need more practitioners. More people out in the world holding these sacred spaces, holding these front lines, pollinating the garden of life, restoring the beauty, balance, harmony, and exquisite balance.
Chapter Four

The architecture

The bones of the school. The structures that hold everything else.

The Six Studios

Six disciplines, each its own avenue. Each carries its own foundational ethos, body of knowledge, and skill set.

Mythopoetic Being

Story medicine. The archetypal narrative framework of identity. The maps of personal and ancestral inheritance.

Spirit Mediation

Interdimensional intermediation. Sovereign vessel. Working with the seen and unseen. Dagara divination.

Shamanic Bodywork

Hands-on work on the blueprint of the energetic being. The body as anchor. Trauma-informed somatic clearing.

Ceremonialist Training

The third order — becoming a practitioner in service to others. Container holding. Procedural ritual technology.

Expressive Arts

Voice, breath, movement, sound. The symphony of the room. Toning and vocal release.

Basins of Creation

The inner garden. Daily attunement at the heart-belly center. Sacred seeds tended over years and decades.

The Four Movements

The arc of all learning, within every studio.

  1. The Map — the known and unknown maps, all the maps that have existed before, synthesized through your own embodied lived experience
  2. Navigating the Map — symbols, terrains, principles, the applied equations of the work
  3. Walking the Terrain — first-person experiential exploration
  4. Field Training — expressing the work in community, in service to others

Running alongside: theory, practicum, tests, initiation, refinement, and continual mentorship.

The Three Orders

The trajectory of the practitioner.

  1. For yourself — clearing, attuning, becoming fluent, ceremony as a rhythm of your own life
  2. On behalf of intentions between the worlds — journeying with purpose, opening here, going into the other world, receiving, bringing back, implementing
  3. In service to others — the most advanced work, where ceremonialist training occurs

The Three Depths

Entry points to the work.

  • Theory — to learn the concepts
  • Embody — to take it into one’s own life
  • Service — to change one’s life and how one affects others

The Fluency Cycle

The rhythm of the work — six steps, moving wider with each cycle.

  1. The mundane self. Hold your water. Attune. Come back.
  2. Communicate clearly. Be communicated with clearly. Develop the skill. Come back.
  3. Bring the identity and ego structure along — ballast, pendulum acclimating to higher oscillation
  4. Etiquette and reciprocity in the other world. Conversation as a technician
  5. Care for the human self navigating these huge changes
  6. Bring instruction back. Receive from the other world. Implement here.

The Anchoring — the heart of the discipline

It is easy to go into the other world. The hard work is implementing what comes through into this world.

We are not only meant to open up to those energies. We are meant to anchor them down. That is what this world needs at this time — an anchoring down of a new reality that we are shaping and co-creating together.

The technical articulation: voltage and amplitude. When the voltage of what is coming through exceeds the body’s grounding capacity, the system overloads and purges to recalibrate. The proactive practice is matching the incoming energy with a body wave that lands and grounds it — undulating the spine in S-curves, finding the slower rhythm beneath the fast one.

The Sovereign Vessel

A core ethical principle: dignity with non-human intelligences.

You’re sharing consciousness in my vessel. I want more dignity. I want more modesty. Can you be more gentle with me?

The medicine — and other intelligences — are adaptive. If you communicate what you need with respect, they will meet you on that ground.

Conversation as Participation

You can within yourself communicate clearly with the intelligence you are sitting with — linguistically, internally.

Speak to me in a language I already understand.

The beings tending to us in this growth will adapt and learn how to be with us.

Power and Agency in the Unseen

You are the one with the human body. You are the one incarnate on the material realm. You are the one with power. Sometimes beings want us to forget that — to give it away to them.

Don’t make meaning while it’s happening

It’s like starting the Lord of the Rings and going, “Oh, I get it.” You have no idea. Let it conclude. Let it land. Let it marinate in the weeks and months to come.

Chapter Seven

Cycles of integration

The work of a ceremony is not the event. It is the opening of a gateway one returns to.

  • Sacred seeds — years to bloom. Some teachings take years or decades. The practitioner tends the garden.
  • Depletion as spaciousness. Fatigue after deep work is the room made for what comes next.
  • Daily morning practice. Heart-belly center. Ask to be guided. Set the day’s intentions.
  • Whimsy as exercise. Practice the will of creation where the stakes are low. A rainbow. A butterfly. A flute.
  • Totemic anchoring. A piece — jewelry, an altar item — imbued with the energy of the threshold crossed.
  • Letter to your future self. From the richness of the heart. Read it back when you are in need.
  • Required integration call. Within one to four weeks of any deep ceremonial work.
  • The world will feel different — that is the point.
  • Open the container, do not close it. A ceremony does not end. It is released into the life of the practitioner.

The voice

The operating system for everything written for or as The Animist Apothecary.

The tonal range — full spectrum

The voice holds two ends at once, and the fluctuation between them is the voice.

At one end: silly, light, life-is-fantastic. Real laughter. Warmth. The body in joy.

Language structure

Cadence: short declarative sentences for principle. Longer flowing sentences for description. Paragraph-as-sentence for emphasis. Triadic phrasings for ceremonial weight.

Punctuation: em dashes are kept — they are the rhythm of Adi Marie’s writing. Italic emphasis carries voice signature on words that matter, used sparingly. Periods are heavy — a sentence ends when the principle is complete.

Paragraph structure: begin with the principle, then unfold. Use rhetorical pauses (single-sentence paragraphs). Close with a soft turn.

Paradoxical pairs: the voice often holds two things at once — compassionately fierce, unprecedented in ancient ground, benevolent and rigorous.

Spacing & density

Generous breathing room. Every section has padding ≥ 5rem top/bottom. Body copy at maximum 720px line length. Cards at 2–4 column grids with 2–3rem gap. Density is earned through content depth, never inflated.

Decorative system

Hairline gold rules between eyebrow and headline. Short horizontal hairlines (40–60px) in Gold. Quote glyph: &ldquo; left double quote, not em dash. Arrows: → (U+2192), never > or &rarr; raw.

Pillar icons as ascending marks (·, ··, ···). No illustrative icons.

Photography direction

Hero imagery: 16:9 cinematic. Golden-hour Topanga canyon, candle-lit interior with earthy wall, forest canopy with sunbeams, hand on knee in stillness. Moody low-key OR golden-hour high-key. Never fluorescent.

Section imagery: ceremonial objects — smoke and moss, oil lamp, brass chalice, woven cloth, fern over labyrinth stone, oak branch with sunlight, lavender, white sage smoke.

Practitioner photography: studio-quality portraits with single-light direction, soft falloff, ceremonial wardrobe.

No stock imagery. No clinic settings. No abstract gradients. Nothing SaaS or wellness-app coded.
