Stage One

The cornerstone chosen

Mythopoetic Being is the studio of narrative as ceremonial practice — attending the story being lived, and choosing differently when the chapter asks for it.

Why this one, of the six.

  • It is the most accessible portal. Everyone has a story. Mythopoetic Being meets a person where they already are — in their inherited narrative — and gives them the tools to choose differently. The threshold of entry is low; the depth is unlimited.
  • It bridges the inner and the ceremonial. Story is the medium through which inner shifts become outer practice. A person who has rewritten the myth they live by has done foundational ceremonial work — they have shaped reality by attending to language.
  • It builds on universal terrain. Every culture has its myths. Every person has a story. No specialized prior knowledge is required — the lineages of myth, archetype, and narrative arc are pan-human.
  • It seeds the language of the school. Mythopoetic Being graduates carry the school’s worldview into their writing, therapy, facilitation, and counsel. They become the school’s first storytellers in the world.
  • It is the studio Adi has been quietly teaching for years. Through one-on-one Pláticas and ceremonial counsel, the work of attending the story being lived is already a thread in Adi’s practice. The curriculum is half-written before you write it.

This sample stands as the shape. The curriculum below is a serious starting point in the studio’s essence — each Quarter’s theory, practice, and initiation drawn from the lineage of narrative work as ceremony. Refine in your voice.

Stage Two

The cohort container

The first cohort sets the shape of all the cohorts that come after. These are the load-bearing decisions.

Name

Mythopoetic Being — Inaugural Cohort

The first cohort is named for what it is. Future cohorts may be named for the season or year they convene in.

Duration

One Wheel

Twelve months, equinox to equinox. Long enough for genuine transformation. Short enough to commit to fully.

Format

Hybrid

Weekly online practice circles, monthly online intensives, four in-person retreats anchored to the equinoxes and solstices.

Size

Eight students

Min six, max nine. The size of a held container — intimate enough to know each person deeply, large enough for the cohort field to teach.

Rhythm

Weekly · Monthly · Quarterly

Weekly online practice circle (90 min). Monthly online intensive (full Saturday). Quarterly in-person retreat (3–4 nights). Integration calls every 3–4 weeks.

Application

Written + intake call

Written application (essay questions). Required intake call with Adi Marie at the standard Sacred Reciprocity rate. Selection by composition, not first-come.

Cohort Dates

September Equinox

Cohort begins at the September equinox, closes at the September equinox one year later. Applications open in early summer, close in mid-August.

Contact Hours

~ 180 hours

~52 weekly circles, 11 monthly intensives, 4 quarterly retreats, plus integration calls and assisting hours. A serious commitment.

Stage Three · Part One

The developmental arc

What transformation moves through a student in twelve months. The arc the curriculum is designed to hold.

Entry · Month 0

The Called One

A person with a calling. Some practice already in motion — therapy, coaching, healing arts, ceremonial sitting. Senses they are meant to hold space for others but does not yet have the form. Comes seeking the bones.

Q1 · Months 1–3

The Initiated

The sovereign self established. Daily practice grounded. Lineage attunement begun. The student now stands in the field of the cohort, the field of the lineage, and the field of their own awakening medicine. The Map is becoming theirs.

Q2 · Months 4–6

The Apprentice

Technical skill emerging. They can read the field, recognize voltage and amplitude, support a held container without dropping the thread. They have assisted in ceremony. Their individual specialty begins to surface. They navigate.

Q3 · Months 7–9

The Walker

The practitioner’s stance integrating. Ethical foundation solid. They have run their first witnessed ritual outside the cohort. Their unique thread is articulating — the specific terrain they were born to walk. They walk the terrain.

Q4 · Months 10–12

The Steward

A ceremonialist now emerging in their own context. Their own community knows them as the one who holds. Their sustainable rhythm is set. They have entered the amakua field of the school. They serve.

Exit · Beyond

The Lineage Carrier

A practitioner who holds ceremonial work in their own life and community, in service to others, with ongoing relationship to the lineage and the school. They are now a node in the field. What flows through them flows through the lineage now.

Stage Three · Part Two

The syllabus

Each quarter anchors one of the four movements. Theory and practice braided. Each closes with an initiation that marks the threshold crossed.

Quarter One · The Map

Becoming initiated to the landscape of story

The student is introduced to the architecture of narrative. The river of story they are already swimming in. The maps that have come before, received.

Theory
  • The animist view of story — what shapes that is invisible
  • Inherited narratives — family, culture, ancestral, era
  • Story as a medium of consciousness
  • The architecture of myth — character, conflict, threshold, return
  • Archetypes as living patterns
  • The four directional arcs of story (descent, ascent, crossing, return)
  • Story as ceremony, not entertainment
Practice
  • The Listening practice — attending the story being lived
  • The Genealogy of Story — mapping the narrative lineages you inherited
  • The Daily Story — writing the day as if it were a chapter of myth
  • Cohort circles — story-sharing as field-building
  • Beginning the Practitioner Journal
Reading
  • Mythic foundational texts (Campbell, Estés, Kalsched — curated list)
  • Monthly written reflection, integrated in next intensive
Quarter One Initiation

Naming Your Genre

At the close of the opening retreat. The student articulates the genre of their current life-story and what is being asked of the protagonist. The first ceremonial act of authorship.

Quarter Two · Navigating the Map

Learning the mechanics of story-shaping

The student learns how a story is changed — not by force, but by attention. The seven tools of story-work. The mechanics of narrative shift.

Theory
  • The shift point — where a story changes course
  • The seven tools (naming, reframing, re-locating, retelling, ritualizing, embodying, releasing)
  • The function of antagonist — what holds a story in place
  • The threshold guardian — what blocks the next chapter
  • The shadow character — what your story refuses to integrate
  • Mythic time vs chronological time of a life
  • The architecture of a turning point
Practice
  • Sitting with one inherited story per week, applying a different tool
  • The Rewrite practice — shifting one variable in a story you tell
  • Cohort witness — telling your shifted story to one peer
  • Daily attending — the morning question: "what story am I in right now?"
  • Integration calls every 3–4 weeks
Reading
  • Texts on narrative therapy, story-shaping practice
  • Continued reflective writing focused on story-mechanics
Quarter Two Initiation

First Re-telling

At the winter retreat, each student tells one story from their life THREE times, each time shaped by a different tool. The cohort witnesses. The student integrates what they noticed about which tool brought which shift.

Quarter Three · Walking the Terrain

Living the chosen story

The student commits to a chosen narrative arc and begins to live as the protagonist. The story becomes the actual shape of the year.

Theory
  • The vows of authorship — what commitment to a story requires
  • Living-as-if — the ethics of inhabiting a story you have chosen
  • The role of community in story-making
  • Witnessing as the practice that holds a new story in place
  • The difference between mythic living and magical thinking
  • Sustaining a new story through the inevitable break
  • Story and the body — how narrative becomes embodied
Practice
  • Declaring the chosen story to the cohort at spring retreat
  • 90-day living-as-if commitment with weekly check-ins
  • Daily ritual that anchors the chosen story (a small daily act)
  • First witnessing of another’s story — holding space for one peer
  • Beginning to write the chosen story in fragments
Reading
  • Texts on narrative therapy, mythic living, story-as-practice
  • Reflective writing in the voice of the chosen story
Quarter Three Initiation

The Threshold Crossing

At the spring retreat, each student takes a literal ceremonial walk across a threshold (real, physical, named) as the formal entry into the chosen story. Witnessed by the cohort. Marked.

Quarter Four · Field Training

Story-holding for others

The student offers story-work to people outside the cohort. They become the one who attends a story for someone else. The graduating practitioner.

Theory
  • The ethics of story-holding — what you do and don’t do for another’s narrative
  • The boundary between counsel and counseling
  • Plática as a form — the cultural lineages of holding-counsel through story
  • Trauma-informed story-work — what’s in scope and what’s not
  • The frame of a story-holding session (opening, listening, offering, closing)
  • Building a story-work practice in your life and community
Practice
  • Three witnessed story-holding sessions with peers (in supervision)
  • One unsupervised session with someone outside the cohort
  • Designing your own ongoing story-practice for after the cohort
  • Final portfolio — methodology articulated in your own voice
  • Cohort sharing — what each student is carrying forward
Reading
  • Texts on facilitation, ethical narrative practice
  • Final reflective essay — the practitioner statement
Quarter Four Initiation

Graduation — Entry into the Amakua

The closing retreat is the graduation ceremony. Formal recognition as a Mythopoetic Being practitioner of The Animist Apothecary. Entry into the lineage field. The student is now a contributor to the amakua.

A trusted body worker or somatic specialist. Holds the embodiment thread through the year. Supports the trauma-informed dimension of the work.

Guest Teachers

2–3 across the year

Brought in for specific transmissions — a lineage holder, a peer practitioner in a complementary tradition, a specialist on a particular dimension of the work.

Administrative Lead

To be named

Handles applications, payments, scheduling, communications, retreat logistics, materials distribution, and the student portal.

Student Liaison

To be named

First point of contact between sessions. Coordinates integration call scheduling. Holds the day-to-day relationship with the cohort so that Adi Marie can hold the depth.

For the inaugural cohort, several roles may be held by the same person or by Adi Marie herself. The structure expands as subsequent cohorts begin to overlap.

  • Integration call — every 3–4 weeks per student, with Adi Marie or a co-facilitator

Notes on the Sample

What this is, and what it isn’t

This is a working sample. It is a real, concrete shape — not a placeholder — but every load-bearing decision in it is yours to confirm, adjust, or refuse.

The Sacred Reciprocity tiers are drawn against the high tier of comparable year-long ceremonial trainings in the field; they can be raised, lowered, or restructured.

The dates assume a September 2026 cohort opening. If that timeline is too compressed, the same architecture maps cleanly to September 2027 with another full year to build the curriculum in detail.

The studio named here as Mythopoetic Being is one studio of six. Once the inaugural cohort runs and closes, its lessons inform the design of the next studios in the school.

The sample is a beginning, not a commitment. The commitment comes when a date is set.
